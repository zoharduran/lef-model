Moon Births

One mechanism for the births of moons, tested planet by planet across the Solar System

Zohar Duran · Independent Researcher, Ramla, Israel · ORCID 0009-0005-3194-9387 · 9 November 2026

Abstract

Most moons of the Solar System orbit close to the equatorial plane of their parent, and many sit close to the corotation line, the height at which a body turning with the planet moves at orbital speed. In this paper we propose that every regular moon was born in the same way: from its parent, at the corotation line, in a window opened and closed by the parent’s differentiation. One parameter sets where the line stands in units of the parent’s radius — the ratio of the spin to the density — so one ruler serves all bodies.

Three modes of birth follow from where a planet stands. Our Moon was built on the tip of a pear and is the only built moon. From Mars to Neptune moons were ejected, loaded with water, from vents raised by internal pressure. At the outer edge Pluto and Charon split from one body; the split was a break of a frozen crust over a soft interior, and the smooth plain of Charon that faces Pluto is the interior exposed in the break.

Moon births depend on distance from the Sun in the shape of an arc. Close to the Sun planets dry fast and the window closes: Mercury and Venus bore no moon. Farther out, planets stay light and wet and eject more moons, up to a peak at Saturn; beyond it frozen surfaces make fewer. A protocol of thirteen checks is applied to every planet and five dwarf planets, a rule fixed in advance separates captured moons, and a matrix shows every body against every condition. What remains open is collected for further research.
