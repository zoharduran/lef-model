01 · THE MYSTERY

The mystery: what did the far-side samples of Chang'e-6 reveal, and how did the heat climb over the Earth's gravity barrier?

In June 2024 the Chinese Chang'e-6 mission returned the first soil samples in history from the far side of the Moon — from the Apollo basin in north-eastern SPA (41.625°S, 206.022°E, about 11.6° from the centre of the dome and 210 km from the crater minimum). The laboratory findings, together with gravity and magnetic data from orbit, reveal a chain of discoveries that a momentary asteroid impact struggles to unify:

  1. A dense mass buried at depth (2.18 × 10¹⁸ kg, B10): beneath the southern half of the central mare area (62°S, 200°E, over an area of ~ 444,500 km², equivalent radius 12.4°) hides a diffuse excess mass equivalent to a metal sphere ~ 95 km across — which did not sink into the Moon's core!
  2. A "baked" mantle 13 times poorer in oxygen, with heavy isotopes of potassium and iron (B11–B13): the source of the Chang'e-6 basalt is completely dry, ~ 13 times poorer in available oxygen than the near side (ΔIW=−1.93±0.58\Delta\mathrm{IW} = -1.93 \pm 0.58 against −0.80 ± 0.64), depleted in KREEP elements (μ≈360\mu \approx 360 at an age of 2.8 Ga against μ≈1,620\mu \approx 1,620 in an ancient 4.203 Ga basalt in the same area!), and carries isotopically heavy iron (δ56Fe=0.13–0.21\delta^{56}\text{Fe} = 0.13\text{–}0.21‰) together with heavy potassium (δ41K=0\delta^{41}\text{K} = 0 to +0.09‰).
  3. Magnetism erased at the centre and strong on the trailing side only, and the potential barrier of 26.2 MJ/kg (B16–D.3): at the centre of SPA the magnetism is almost zero (0.8% of the area above 5 nT), and all the strong magnetism (32%–42%, peak ~ 22 nT near Mare Ingenii and the bright swirls) is concentrated in the north-west (the trailing side) — on the same side as the thorium crescent, but shifted about 8° north of it. And in addition: how did the heat and material from the Earth manage to climb over the potential barrier (26.2 MJ/kg) to the far side?
02 · AN IMAGE

An image: a sauce reducing over a low flame, a tea bag steeped again and again, and a continuous siphon

Four everyday images illuminate the physics and chemistry of the depths of SPA in the LEF model:

  • A sauce reducing over a low flame (the concentration of iron in place without sinking, B10, B12): unlike an active mare, in which material rises and falls and can sweep lumps of iron down to the core (as in Mare Moscoviense), SPA was not an active mare — there was no cyclic up-and-down flow. Material rich in metallic iron that arrived from the Earth at the base of SPA was heated and baked in place: the metallic iron "drank" and absorbed the oxygen around it (lowering ΔIW\Delta\mathrm{IW} to −1.93), and when the heat evaporated the other materials, the iron that did not evaporate concentrated in place and froze as the area cooled!
  • A tea bag steeped again and again (the shift from an enriched source at 4.20 Ga to a depleted source at 2.81 Ga, B13): the material that arrived from the Earth through the pear landed rich in KREEP elements (μ≈1,620\mu \approx 1,620 at 4.203 Ga). Prolonged heating from below melted the mantle again and again and each time removed the first melt (which carried the thorium with it to the crescent), until a "washed-out" residue resistant to melting remained (μ≈360\mu \approx 360 at 2.807 Ga) — which also explains why only tiny amounts of late lava erupted!
  • Milk poured into coffee and frozen before it mixed (the bright swirls of Mare Ingenii against Moscoviense, B17): when the two-material stream — iron from below and alumina-silica from above — burst out and froze quickly under the Moon's rotation (Ekman number ≪1\ll 1), the elongated grains stood vertically in the feeding conduit (dark iron-rich lanes) and lay horizontally in the spreading (bright plagioclase-rich loops), whereas in active Moscoviense the iron sank before freezing, so neither a magnetic field (1.3 nT, 0% above 5 nT) nor swirls formed there!
  • A continuous siphon over the top of a hill (crossing the potential barrier, D.3–D.3a): a single parcel of material launched alone at 1.8–2.6 km/s (1.6–3.4 MJ/kg) would fall back before the corotation line (26.2 MJ/kg). But here we are dealing with a continuous body full of material pushed from below by a source pressure of ~ 150 GPa (Paper 3): the moment its upper part passes the corotation line and is drawn outward by the rotation, it lowers the pressure at the base and draws more material from below like a siphon — without the pressure ever becoming negative!
03 · SCIENCE AND MODEL

Science and model: the eight deep anomalies (B10–B17) and the antipode geometry

Parts B10–B17 and C of Paper 08 close the remaining of the 17 anomalies of SPA and the map of centres on the far side:

  1. The rigid-mirror test against the map of the three groups of centres (Part C): a rigid copy (a "stamp") of the rings and maria of Imbrium and Serenitatis onto their antipodes gives a narrow ellipse (2,144 × 1,340 km, axis ratio 1.60) that does not match the shape of SPA (2,402 × 2,056 km, ratio 1.17) — and this negative result is kept explicitly, to make clear that the burner determined where the craters were erased, while the outline of the basin was set by the collapse of the dome. When all the centres are placed on one map (Figure C.1), an orderly row from north to south in three groups emerges:
    • (a) In the north — the two exits of the burner: the Imbrium antipode (37.6°S, 160.0°E, with the thin-crust dome of Ingenii 5.7° away above it) and the Serenitatis antipode (27.3°S, 198.4°E, with the Apollo dome 10° away above it).
    • (b) In the centre — the focus of heat and crater erasure: the centre of the dome/inner ring (51.4°S, 196.8°E), the centre of the ellipse (53°S, 191°E), the nearby magnetic peak (51.0°S, 190.0°E) and the crater minimum (45.4°S, 197.9°E) — all within 6° of the centre of the dome!
    • (c) In the south — the heavy structural focus: the lowest floor (61°S, 199°E), the dense mass (62°S, 200°E) and the thinnest crust (61°S, 184°E) — all within ~ 230 km of each other!
  2. Why does the dome sit south of the two exits? — the horizontal push in the pear and the rounding of the body (§C.3–C.4): the tip of the pear on which the Moon sat stood at latitude ~ 22.5°N, while its base sat around the equator. Inside a rotating body, the centrifugal force acts perpendicular to the rotation axis (horizontally) and not along the line to the Earth's centre — so the stream entered the contact disc north of its centre (where Imbrium and Serenitatis lie) and exited on the far side up to ~ 22.5° south of the opposite point! In the flattened stage (1 : 0.637 : 0.601), the centre of the dome sat 17.0° south of the Serenitatis exit and 21.2° south-east of the Imbrium exit; and afterwards the rounding into a sphere (Chapters 13–14) widened these angular distances to exactly the 24.1° and 29.2° measured today!
04 · THE MECHANISM IN WORDS

The mechanism in words: answering the seven challenges (E.1–E.7) and mapping the shift from flattening to sphere

Parts C.3 and E of Paper 08 provide precise quantitative answers to all seven questions and challenges raised by the recent scientific literature (including the Chang'e-6 findings of 2025–2026):

  1. Iridium and gold in the Chang'e-6 regolith (E.1): the metal grains bearing Ir and Au found by Liu et al. (2026) were found inside undated basalt and anorthosite fragments in the surface regolith (late space dust gathered over billions of years, which the prediction explicitly allows), and not inside the dated 4.25 Ga norites. Moreover, elemental ratios of Ir and Au do not distinguish between metal from the Earth's depths and an asteroid — only an isotope measurement (Cr, Ti, Mo, Ru, Os) decides.
  2. The dense mass, the magnetic peaks and the thin crust are not beneath the crater minimum (E.3–E.5): the minimum (45.4°S, 197.9°E) marks where the heat erased craters at the surface in the direction of the Serenitatis exit; the magnetic peak (51.0°S, 190.0°E, 230 km away) marks the edges of the hot core, where the magnetism was not erased above the Curie temperature (at the centre the field is only 0.85–1.9 nT); and the dense mass (62°S, 200°E) sits in the deep structural south of the basin.
  3. Buried basalt as the cause of the shallow craters (E.6): in the LEF model the buried basalt (8.7 × 10⁴ km²) is a product of the burner and not its rival — and the decisive test is that the shallowness of the craters continues even outside the buried-basalt areas, because of the compaction of the baked ground.
  4. The geometric shift in the rounding from flattening to sphere (table §C.3): in the transition from the elongated body (1 : 0.637 : 0.601) to today's sphere, no point slid across the ground, but the angles relative to the tip of the long axis opened up: the Imbrium exit moved from 32.3°S, 158.1°E to 37.6°S, 160.0°E (a change of 5.5°), the Serenitatis exit moved from 27.4°S, 185.1°E to 27.3°S, 198.4°E (11.8°), the centre of the dome moved from 44.0°S, 180.8°E to 51.4°S, 196.8°E (13.0°), and the crater minimum from 39.9°S, 182.5°E to 45.4°S, 197.9°E (12.6°).

06 · THE SIGNATURE

The signature in reality: 16 of 17 anomalies matched — and the open predictions

In the summary of the systematic comparison in section B18 of Paper 08: of the 17 anomalies of SPA, mainstream science fully explains 6, partly 9, barely 1 (the prolonged volcanism over 2 billion years), and 0 (does not explain at all) the pattern of the crater record at the centre of the basin. By contrast, the burner mechanism of the LEF model matches at once 16 of the 17 anomalies, and 1 partly (B14), awaiting the isotope test:

  • The sharpest prediction for a future laboratory measurement (Part F): measuring the isotopes of Cr, Ti, Mo, Ru, Os in the metal grains inside the two dated Chang'e-6 norites (`CE6C0100YJFM002-158` and `CE6C0200YJFM001EGP07-16`) will show a pure terrestrial signature with no meteoritic deviation.
  • Further predictions testable from orbit: (1) a concentration of floor-fractured craters inside the central deficit area; (2) the shallowness of the craters persisting even outside the buried-basalt areas; (3) a radial gradient of volatile loss and oxygen reduction (ΔIW\Delta\mathrm{IW}) strongest around the minimum point and weakening toward the rim; and (4) a complete absence of lava in SPA younger than ~ 1.5 Ga.
